Most Entrepreneurs Stay Broke Because They Do This (And You’re Probably Doing It Too)

Encourages entrepreneurs to focus on certification, conversations, referrals, activity tracking, and team development in a funding business.

Jason Feimster · Moonshine Capital

ORIGINAL LINKEDIN EDITION ↗
Originally published 2025-05-17. Historical offers, program terms, and results reflect the original article and may have changed. Some original illustrations are linked rather than hosted. Moonshine Capital may receive affiliate compensation; no funding or income outcome is guaranteed.

If you're an entrepreneur, freelancer, agency owner, or self-employed side-hustler still wondering why your calendar is full but your Stripe account is starving—this is your wake-up call.

The following article distills the unfiltered fire from the DAC Agent Channel's “Agent Update 5/16/25” (watch here) into a no-holds-barred manifesto for anyone ready to stop playing business and start making bank. This isn’t corporate fluff. It’s tactical gospel for those ready to trade excuses for execution.

Let's get surgical.

PART 1: You’re Not Just Selling Money—You’re Selling Freedom (But You Forgot)

Let’s talk about the disease eating away at your business like a termite in a beach house: distraction masked as productivity. You show up to meetings, check Slack, drop a few LinkedIn posts like a digital peacock, but let’s be honest—your bank account still looks like it’s in a medically induced coma.

That’s where this Agent Update kicks you square in the hustle. What you heard wasn’t just an update. It was a brutal wake-up call from the entrepreneurial trenches. It reminded you that if you’re in business funding, or any client-serving industry, you’re not selling a “product”—you’re selling permission.

Permission for your clients to stop treading water. Permission for them to scale up, hire help, maybe finally take a damn vacation without checking their sales dashboard every 20 minutes. You’re selling freedom, but most of you have forgotten that. Why? Because you’re too wrapped up in what you’re trying to make.

Wake up. Reframe.

You're not just in funding. You're in impact. The faster you get that through your skull, the faster you start winning. Because let’s be clear: real entrepreneurs don’t chase commissions. They chase transformation. The moment you start treating your services like a lifeline for businesses on the brink (because, let’s be honest, they are), you stop begging for attention and start commanding it.

"Your consistency does create opportunity for you to have success."

That’s not a motivational poster. That’s gospel. Most people fail not because they lack talent—but because they treat opportunity like a part-time hobby. You, reading this right now, probably have 40 unread DMs from leads who didn’t respond—and you haven’t followed up. But hey, maybe next week you’ll post about mindset again, right?

Let’s talk incentives. DAC just dropped a preferred product incentive that gives agents 10% extra commission—$5,000 turns into $5,500. $7,000 turns into $7,700. Sounds sweet, right? But if your only excitement comes from how much you make and not how fast your clients grow, you’re missing the point. The commissions are a symptom of something bigger: you finally doing your job right.

And the job? It’s not pitching. It’s not even selling. It’s understanding exactly why a small business owner—tired, stressed, knee-deep in payroll hell—needs a funding solution today, not tomorrow. You’re the emergency room for entrepreneurs. Show up like it.

Let’s spotlight the brutal truth about prepayment discounts. This is where some of you glaze over because “math is hard.” But pay attention because this is the value you’re leaving on the table when you half-explain your offers. Let’s say a client borrows $100k and owes $128k over 12 months. They pay off early? Boom—massive discounts. Could be $6,000. Could be more. In some cases, with 100% prepayment discounts, they’re shaving off the entire interest.

You tell me—how many banks are offering that? I’ll wait.

Still thinking about that commission? Think again. Because when you break down these funding options like a pro, you start building trust. And trust builds volume. And volume builds your damn empire.

PART 2: Why Most Agents Fail (And Why You Might Be One of Them)

Let’s rip the Band-Aid off: most agents—and I mean most—are glorified link-sharers. You’re out here dropping application links like pennies in a wishing well, hoping one turns into a six-figure client. You’re not a strategist. You’re not a consultant. You’re a walking spam folder.

Too harsh? Then this part is for you.

The Agent Update made one thing clear: activity creates aptivity. Yeah, it's a made-up word—but it's dead-on. You can have all the knowledge in the world about prepayment discounts and FICO thresholds, but if you’re not talking to people, it’s game over. Business doesn't come to you because you posted a Canva graphic with a recycled motivational quote. It comes to you because you got uncomfortable enough to ask your cousin’s landscaping guy if he wants $250K at 0% interest.

And if you’re too proud—or too scared—to pitch to your own warm network, let me translate what you're really saying: I’d rather be broke than be temporarily uncomfortable.

“Saying you won’t talk to local business owners is like saying you want to get fit without changing your diet.”

Exactly. You’re the guy refusing to eat vegetables but still wondering why your abs aren’t popping. You want the reward without the rejection. But here's the twist: rejection isn't the enemy. Apathy is. And right now, your apathy is dressed up in a hoodie that says “I’m working on my brand.”

Stop branding. Start building.

There’s a reason someone named Bassett showed up on Tuesday and got two funding approvals by Friday. This isn’t Hogwarts. There’s no secret spell. He just did the damn work. And if a newbie can land $180,000 in funding in less than five days, what’s your excuse?

Now let’s address another red flag in this space: the DIY isolationist. You know who you are. You saw the social media update, but you didn't repost it because you "want to keep your page clean." Your ego's curated aesthetic is costing you real leads. You didn’t even bother to ask, “How do I reshare this from Facebook to Instagram?” No shame—just lazy. And broke.

The irony? Sharon (yes, someone’s mom) reposted a DAC post and got a client out of it. Your mom’s probably out-recruiting you.

Still think you’re too good for Facebook?

And let’s talk about recruiting. Not as an afterthought. As a survival tactic. You're not just earning 10% override commissions on your direct agents. You’re building a compound engine where every agent is a node in your revenue network. Every time you dodge recruiting because "you’re not into MLM," you're leaving a pile of long-term residuals on the table. Residuals that don’t care if your funnel is working this week.

You want freedom? Recruit. You want passive income? Train. You want scalability? Multiply. Period.

The company even threw in a leaderboard to gamify it. Top agents like Wade Cordell and Ellen Win are showing up and taking names. Why? Because they understand that a team outperforms a solo hustler every single time. It's math, not magic.

Let’s call out the final failing trait: thinking you’re too experienced to learn anything new. LMS certifications are rolling out soon. Everyone—and I mean everyone—is required to complete them. The old heads muttering “I already know all this” will be the first ones blindsided when someone half their age passes them up in commissions and client volume because they’re doing the work.

Certify or get sidelined.

And remember: if you're not certified, you still get paid—but way less. So go ahead and ignore the training. That’s the equivalent of running a marathon in flip-flops and wondering why your feet are bleeding.

Pause here, breathe—and then ask yourself: Do I want more?

PART 3: The Money Is in the Message—And You’re Probably Saying the Wrong Thing

By now, you’ve either punched a wall in agreement or rage-quit this article. Either way, congrats—you’re feeling something. And that’s more than most of your leads do when they read your half-hearted outreach messages.

Let’s fix that, shall we?

Your messaging sucks. There, I said it. You’re either too vague (“We help businesses get funding”) or too spammy (“$250K in 24 hours! No credit check!!!”). Both extremes trigger the same reflex in your audience: delete, block, ignore.

Here’s the brutal truth: the money you want is hiding inside the conversations you're avoiding. You’re afraid to message your cousin who owns a restaurant. You’re scared to approach the guy from your gym who runs a roofing company. Why? Because you think it’ll make you look desperate.

Here’s what actually makes you look desperate: being broke.

Now, the update dropped some heat about how to use preferred industry scripts—and honestly, if you’re not following this to the letter, you’re freelancing your failure. They even laid it out for you:

“Hey Lisa, just wanted to reach out because as you know, I’m in business funding… we have a special program right now for salons like yours…”

Boom. Personalized, industry-specific, and concise. That’s not a pitch. That’s an offer with context. And context is everything.

Your warm market—people who know you, like you, trust you—is the lowest-friction money on the planet. Yet you’re out here chasing strangers in DMs instead of texting your aunt who runs a successful cleaning service.

But fine. Let’s say your warm market is a barren wasteland of unemployed friends and that one guy who still sells essential oils. Cool. Move to your local market. And no, that doesn’t mean building a $12,000 funnel. It means walking down Main Street, seeing a landscaping truck, and picking up your phone:

“Hey, I saw your truck and wanted to introduce myself. I’m in business funding and we help landscaping companies get capital when banks say no.”

Short. Punchy. Human. And if you say you’re “not good on the phone,” go cry about it while Sharon closes another deal off a Facebook post.

Want to go national? Fine. But pick a damn industry. Become the guy for roofers. The gal for gym owners. The funding whisperer for HVAC businesses. You don’t need to fund the entire world. You need to dominate one vertical and let the word-of-mouth do its dirty work.

Use your memory jogger. Go through your contacts. Who do you know in construction? Write it down. Who do you know in retail, food service, trucking? Write. It. Down. Build your list like your life depends on it—because financially, it kind of does.

The best part? You don’t even have to do the heavy lifting. ChatGPT (yeah, that’s me) can generate a list of home health businesses in your zip code with a single prompt. You can find leads faster than you find Netflix shows you’ll never finish.

Now for the nukes: prepayment discount structure.

Too many agents are fumbling this. Here's the breakdown you should be screaming from the rooftops:

  • 25% Prepayment Discount: Your client pays back the full principal but gets a 25% discount on the interest if they pay off early. On a $100K loan with $28K interest, they could save nearly $6K. That’s real money.
  • 100% Prepayment Discount: Your client pays zero interest if they pay off early. They just return the principal. Banks don’t offer this. Sharks don’t offer this. You do. And if that’s not a lead magnet, you need to go back to selling candles on Etsy.

Also, stop relying on “minimum qualifications” as gospel. Yes, the sheet says 50K revenue, 680 FICO, 2 years in business—but that’s a guideline, not a law. DAC is approving fundings for clients under that threshold all the time. You’re gatekeeping your own income because you think underwriting is a vending machine. It’s not. It’s jazz—structured improvisation. So submit every deal that’s even close.

Let’s wrap this section with your to-do list:

  • Text 5 warm contacts daily
  • DM or call 3 local businesses
  • Invite one person to become an agent every day
  • Like, share, and repost DAC social content
  • Track your activity and post it in the Facebook group like it's your touchdown dance

You doing that? Then you’re winning. You skipping it? Then enjoy your motivational memes, because that’s all you’ll have.

PART 4: Stop Worshiping Strategy—Start Worshiping Execution

Let’s get one thing straight right now: you don’t have a strategy problem. You have a doing the damn thing problem.

You want to be the next Grant Cardone, the next big exit, the next entrepreneur-to-influencer pipeline miracle—but you’re still out here scheduling “strategy days” where you sit on Canva redesigning your Instagram highlight covers. Your idea of productivity is a vision board and three hours of keyword research. Spoiler: Google doesn't pay you for procrastinating in high-res.

Here’s what we need to make violently clear: Activity creates income. Activity creates impact. Inactivity creates excuses.

Most of you are hiding in perfection. You’re tweaking your onboarding email sequence while Bassett, the brand-new agent who didn’t even know what DAC stood for on Tuesday, is out here getting approvals by Friday. Why? Because he didn’t sit around building a better strategy. He executed like a savage.

He made calls. Sent texts. Pitched people who had money and needed more. That’s it. No CRM. No “value ladder.” No four-week bootcamp to build his mindset. Just a phone and a product that solves real pain.

This is where we separate the crowd: Doers vs. Delayers.

And you, my friend, need to ask yourself which one you are. Because there’s no more hiding behind “I’m still learning.” You’re not learning—you’re loitering. You’re not stuck—you’re stalling. You’re not underqualified—you’re undercommitted.

“Every business owner has to deal with people who don’t like their product. That product doesn’t exist that everyone loves.”

Translation: If you’re waiting for universal approval before you act, you’ll die broke and liked. Is that your goal?

You’re going to get ghosted. You’re going to get told no. You’re going to have clients who smile in your face and take another offer behind your back. That’s called Tuesday.

But on the flip side, you’ll also find agents who outperform your wildest expectations—and they’ll build your downline while you’re asleep. You’ll close deals while you’re on vacation (if you ever let yourself take one). You’ll earn commissions off someone you introduced who introduced someone who introduced someone else—and all you did was open your mouth.

Let’s pivot hard into reality now: certification is coming.

You may think it’s just another hoop, another boring training. But let me tell you what it actually is: leverage. Because DAC is making certification mandatory to unlock full pay. If you don’t complete it? You’ll still get paid—but just enough to buy a decent burrito. You want the full steak dinner? Do the damn training.

The certification covers everything from how to explain prepayment discounts to how to actually not embarrass yourself on a sales call. And guess what? You’ll be better for it. Because most of you are winging it. You’re putting six-figure revenue goals on a business built like a 2007 blogspot.

And speaking of winging it, let’s talk about agent management.

Some of you still think leading a team means “sending encouragement.” That’s not leadership—that’s passive-aggressive cheerleading. You want your agents to perform? Teach them. Train them. Challenge them. Hold them accountable. And yeah, sometimes you’ll lose them. That’s part of the game. Not everyone deserves to stay in your circle just because they clicked “Enroll.”

You want to really win? Here’s your new religion: Multiply or die trying.

Because the ones making real money aren’t the lone wolves with the perfect pitch. They’re the empire builders who realize one productive agent can be worth more than ten hours of cold calling. It’s chess, not checkers—and if you’re not moving pieces on the board, you’re the pawn.

And let’s not skip this one: clarity over complication.

Clients don’t care about your script. They care about results. They don’t need a funding algorithm. They need money. And fast. So stop giving lectures and start giving options. Start listening. Start offering solutions instead of noise.

One of the most important takeaways from the update?

“Every funding has prepayment discounts. But only you offer the 100% discount.”

You know what that is? Leverage. Start selling it like you mean it.

And here’s a bonus pro-tip straight from the battlefield: Every stalled app is an open door. If you get an alert that someone started but didn’t finish their app, follow up immediately. That’s not a fail—that’s a future deal that needs a nudge. Ask them what held them up. Help them finish it. Don’t let qualified clients rot in your pipeline because you were “busy.”

You want to make real money? Start acting like the funding pro you claim to be.

Certify. Follow up. Close. Repeat.

PART 5: You’re Sitting on a Goldmine (But You’re Using It Like a Sandbox)

Let’s wrap this up by talking about the real elephant in your entrepreneurial living room—the fact that you’ve got everything you need, right now, and you’re still acting like you’re waiting for a miracle.

You’ve got the platform. You’ve got the product. You’ve got a company dropping pre-approved funding with 100% interest discounts like it’s Oprah’s Favorite Things. And yet you’re moving like your business is a side hobby you do between Netflix and doomscrolling.

This isn’t a resources problem. It’s a resolve problem.

You’ve got access to a business model that creates six-figure incomes from home, and you’re out here treating it like a garage sale. The tools are there: a funding platform, replicated websites, daily training calls, automated notifications, even a memory jogger for when your brain runs out of leads (which—let’s be real—is most days).

But none of it matters if you don’t move.

Here’s what the update hammered home: your job is activity. Not mental prep. Not digital feng shui. Not waiting for someone to like your Reel before you send that link.

“Fundings create income and they create impact.”

Yes, impact. That word you keep throwing around in your captions but forgetting in your conversations.

You’re not just getting someone money—they might hire employees with that. Expand locations. Keep the lights on. Avoid bankruptcy. This is not some abstract cash grab—it’s a second chance at survival. And if you can’t show up with enough urgency to care about that, hang it up.

Remember Louie? The business owner who needed 60K to open a second pizza shop but couldn’t qualify for anything? That dude now has two locations because of a DAC agent. Real talk: if that agent had decided to “take the day off,” Louie’s expansion would’ve died on the cutting room floor of the American Dream.

You are someone’s missing link. But only if you act like it.

And speaking of acting—stop waiting for your feelings to catch up. Motivation is a liar. You won’t feel ready. You’ll feel awkward, rejected, tired, discouraged. Do it anyway.

Because no product, no business model, no coaching program will save you if you don’t do what every successful entrepreneur learns: discipline over dopamine.

Let’s run back the action steps from the update and etch them into your soul:

🔥 DAILY ACTION CHECKLIST 🔥

  • Reach out to 5 warm contacts about preferred funding
  • Connect with 3 local businesses per day
  • Invite one person to become an agent (every day, no exceptions)
  • Like, follow, and repost DAC’s official social pages
  • Join (or watch) the 11:30AM daily Zoom call
  • Track and share your Micro Sharing Challenge in the Facebook group

Every single one of these steps is designed to scale your income. You just need to execute like your rent is due. Because it is.

And let’s kill this myth right now: “I’m not a salesy person.” Great. Even better. Because DAC isn’t about selling—it’s about serving. You’re helping small businesses avoid collapse. You’re helping people escape corporate hell with an agency of their own. You’re creating options in a world full of closed doors.

You want legacy? Build it one funding at a time. One agent at a time. One “Yes, I’ll look into that” at a time.

Because here’s the deal no one tells you: no one will believe in your dream more than you do. And if you’re only halfway in, your results will be halfway decent at best.

But if you go all in? You might just wake up a year from now with a six-figure income, a team of high-performing agents, and a new car with “Paid by DAC” on the license plate frame.

But only if you earn it.

20 In-Depth FAQs

  • What is DAC and how does it help entrepreneurs? DAC (David Allen Capital) is a business funding platform that provides capital to small businesses often overlooked by banks. Entrepreneurs can earn commissions by helping businesses get fast funding.
  • What’s the “preferred product incentive” mentioned in the update? It’s a promotion where agents earn 10% more commission on qualified fundings using DAC’s preferred funding options—those offering steep prepayment discounts.
  • How fast can someone get funding through DAC? In many cases, approved clients can receive funding within 24-48 hours. Some have been approved and funded within a single business day.
  • What are the qualifications for preferred funding? Preferred funding typically requires: $30K/month revenue (lowest of last 4 months), 680+ FICO score, 2+ years in business. Higher terms need 100K/month and 700+ FICO.
  • What’s a 100% prepayment discount? If a client pays off their advance early, they owe only the unpaid principal—completely waiving the remaining interest. It’s rare and a massive value-add.
  • How can new agents get started quickly? By focusing on warm market outreach, local business outreach, and consistent social media sharing. Activity is the fastest path to results.
  • Is certification required to be paid? You’ll still get paid without certification, but much less. Certification is required for full commissions and to be considered a “qualified agent.”
  • Can someone work this as a side hustle? Absolutely. Many agents are gig workers, freelancers, and side hustlers who build part-time DAC agencies into full-time incomes.
  • What are the main industries DAC targets? Construction, trades, landscaping, home healthcare, retail, restaurants, and manufacturing are top-performing verticals.
  • How do I follow up with a stalled application? If an applicant doesn’t complete their submission, follow up with a personal call or message to assist them. Many deals are salvaged this way.
  • What makes DAC different from banks? DAC funds businesses that banks often deny. There’s no requirement for collateral, and approvals are faster with fewer hoops.
  • How does DAC pay agents? Agents are paid commissions per funded deal, with overrides on agents they personally recruit, plus promotional bonuses.
  • How big can an agency get? There’s no cap. Some agents build large organizations earning overrides on dozens (or hundreds) of active funding agents.
  • What are micro-sharing points? It’s part of a daily activity tracker where you earn points by taking daily actions—like calls, messages, videos—and share your progress.
  • Can clients use the funds for anything? Yes. Working capital from DAC can be used for payroll, expansion, equipment, renovations, or debt consolidation.
  • Do I need to be a financial expert to join DAC? Nope. You just need the ability to talk to people, follow training, and take action. The platform and training do the heavy lifting.
  • What if a business doesn’t meet preferred criteria? They may still qualify for other DAC programs. Agents are encouraged to submit every lead—even if they don’t hit “perfect” numbers.
  • What’s the role of social media in this business? It’s your digital storefront. Sharing DAC content (and adding your link) builds visibility, trust, and attracts both clients and agents.
  • Are there live trainings I can join? Yes. There are daily Zoom trainings at 11:30 AM ET, plus recordings for those who can’t attend live. These keep you sharp and informed.
  • Can I build recurring income with DAC? Absolutely. By recruiting and developing agents, you earn override commissions for the lifetime of their business activity

Final Reflections

So get certified. Pick up the phone. Send the damn message. Build your team. Share your link. Track your activity. And repeat it all until the life you want stops feeling like a fantasy.

You’re not selling funding. You’re selling freedom. And if you sell it with heart, hustle, and humility—there’s no ceiling to what you can earn or who you can impact.
