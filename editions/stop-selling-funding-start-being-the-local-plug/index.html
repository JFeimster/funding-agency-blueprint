Stop Selling Funding. Start Being The Local Plug.

Explains how local visibility, bank relationships, and practical resources can position a funding broker as a trusted community contact.

Jason Feimster · Moonshine Capital

ORIGINAL LINKEDIN EDITION ↗
Originally published 2026-01-17. Historical offers, program terms, and results reflect the original article and may have changed. Some original illustrations are linked rather than hosted. Moonshine Capital may receive affiliate compensation; no funding or income outcome is guaranteed.

There’s a certain smell that makes business owners recoil faster than a gym bro dodging leg day:

"commission breath"

You know it. They know it. Bank managers definitely know it. And the second a business owner feels like you’re “trying to get yours,” they go full turtle-mode and disappear back into the cashflow abyss.

DAC’s "Special Business Building Night" was basically a clinic on one idea:

If you want referrals, stop chasing referrals. Become the most neighborly, useful human in your town’s business ecosystem.

Not “online guru.” Not “DM blaster.” Not “link dropper.”

A local operator with a mission.

Embedded content: DAC's Special Business Building Night

The Real Reason This Works (And Why Most Brokers Stay Broke)

David laid it out in plain English:

Business owners aren’t thinking about APR calculators and financial theory. They’re thinking:

  • “How do I make payroll Friday?”
  • “How do I keep inventory stocked?”
  • “How do I stop living at my business?”
  • “How do I buy my kid’s Christmas without pretending everything’s fine?”

Six in ten businesses are breaking even or losing money. That’s not a stat you scroll past. That’s a whole civilization running on caffeine, duct tape, and denial.

So when you walk in like a walking commission funnel, they can smell it.

But when you show up like a front-line medic for the front line of capitalism, everything changes.

Local trust compounds. And compounding is the only “get rich” strategy that isn’t a scam.

January Funding Special: Gyms + Health Clubs (This One’s a Layup)

DAC is running a January funding special for gyms and health clubs, designed to make approvals sweeter:

  • Higher approval amounts
  • Lower lender fees or lower total repayment cost
  • One of those combos, and it’s significant

Translation: If you know gym owners, yoga studios, training facilities, recovery centers… this is the month to talk.

And here’s the punchline: David reached out to seven gyms… two were interested immediately. Not because he’s magical. Because incentives + local relationships = easy doors.

Spanish Launch: A Wide-Open Market Most Brokers Won’t Touch

This is one of those “early mover” moments people will brag about later.

Key points:

  • Applications + websites translated to Spanish
  • Spanish-speaking client support team
  • Social posts and printed materials (drop cards, brochures) in Spanish
  • Current Spanish site experience is on Bank Breezy (DAC site translation is coming)

Rough opportunity size shared: There are millions of Hispanic-owned businesses in the U.S., and a meaningful slice are doing the monthly revenue that fits Bank Breezy’s core funding qualifications.

Translation: this is not a niche. It’s a continent.

The move here is simple:

  • If you’re bilingual, you’re holding a cheat code
  • If you’re not bilingual, partner with someone who is
  • Or aim your outreach at areas/business networks where Spanish-first owners operate

No one else is really serving them well. That’s the whole point.

Local Wins. National Transactions.

This was the thesis of the night:

National funding is a lane. Local funding is a moat.

A moat means: competitors look at your market and decide it’s not worth attacking, because you’re entrenched in trust.

David’s version of “moat building” is aggressively unsexy:

  • Chamber of Commerce
  • Ribbon cuttings
  • Local networking
  • Referring business to other local owners
  • Becoming known as “that funding person”

And the stat that should slap you awake:

It takes ~10x more contacts to convert in a “stranger market” than in a local market.

So if you’re doing the national spray-and-pray grind… you’re basically choosing the hard mode.

The 8-Step Local Market Expansion Playbook (Simple, Not Easy)

The outline (in plain language):

  • Get certified and build competence
  • Set and work your plan (daily + weekly activity)
  • Climb the ladder (earn your way up)
  • Pick a tight radius (a real, ownable local market)
  • Canvas with neighborly energy until they know your name
  • Serve clients while team-building (don’t wait)
  • Build 2–10 active brokers locally (you + one buddy scales fast)
  • Duplicate into nearby towns as leaders emerge

This is how you build a market that produces every week without you begging the algorithm for mercy.

Social Media: The Digital Handshake That Works When You Use It Like an Adult

Maguire dropped the clean framework:

Social media is not for selling. It’s for trust-building while you’re not in the room.

The simple rhythm:

  • 3 posts/week

Weekly engagement minimum:

  • Comment on 5 local business posts
  • Like + reply to comments on your stuff

Never pitch in comments. Start conversations privately.

The goal is that when a business owner is spiraling at 1:30am, they stumble onto your page and think:

“This person actually gives a damn.”

That’s it. That’s the whole cheat code.

Bank Relationships: Stop Acting Like Banks Are the Enemy

Banks don’t enjoy saying “no.” They fear losing clients when clients get declined.

Your job is to become the safe alternative:

  • You’re not going to push bad deals
  • You’re not going to embarrass them
  • You’re not going to create backlash

Bank managers will test you with a couple referrals before they trust you.

And if you handle those with integrity (including telling someone “don’t take it” when it’s not a fit), you become the bank’s pressure-release valve.

That’s real power.

Local Leaders: Revenue Share + Equity Talk (The “Builder” Lane)

This part matters if you’re playing the long game.

DAC teased a path for local leaders that includes:

  • Revenue share (piece of monthly revenue)
  • Equity incentive concepts tied to building the company

No hype promises. Just the obvious business truth: builders get rewarded when builders build.

If you want “side hustle money,” cool. If you want to build a compounding asset, local leadership is the lane.

Your No-BS Action Plan From This Call (Do This Now)

Step 1: Pick your anchor town

Stop trying to “serve everyone everywhere.” Own a radius. Be known.

Step 2: Reach out to 7 gyms this week

Use the January special as the reason. Be human.

Step 3: Set up your Facebook business page

Follow the DAC naming + banner rules. Then start the 3-post rhythm.

Step 4: Leave 5 meaningful comments on local business posts

Every week. No pitching. Just neighborly operator energy.

Step 5: Identify 3 banks and schedule coffee chats

Your goal is collaboration, not referrals. Make them feel safe.

Step 6: Move now for March Madness

You don’t need details to start producing.

The Gut Check

If you’re reading this thinking, “Yeah, but I’m not a salesperson…”

Good. Stop trying to be one.

Be the person business owners trust when the bank says “no.”

No commission breath. Just mission.

That’s how you become unavoidable.
