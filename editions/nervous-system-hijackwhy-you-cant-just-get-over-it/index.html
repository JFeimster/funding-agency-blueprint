Nervous System Hijack—Why You Can’t “Just Get Over It"

Discusses the author’s framing of physical stress around rejection and offers exercises intended to make outreach feel less threatening.

Jason Feimster · Moonshine Capital

ORIGINAL LINKEDIN EDITION ↗
Originally published 2025-06-17. Historical offers, program terms, and results reflect the original article and may have changed. Some original illustrations are linked rather than hosted. Moonshine Capital may receive affiliate compensation; no funding or income outcome is guaranteed.

You ever feel your heart rate spike over a freaking follow-up email? Like you’re about to get hit with a cease-and-desist for offering help? Or maybe you re-read that “Not interested, thanks” text and spiral into a shame-coma that ends with Netflix and Doritos and zero productivity for the next 48 hours?

Welcome to your nervous system, hijacked. No, you’re not weak. No, you’re not overly sensitive. You are hardwired—literally—to freak out. And not because you’re broken. Because somewhere along the line, your brain got the message that perceived rejection is a life-threatening event.

Let’s break this down—brutally, biologically, and unapologetically.

Your Brain Thinks Rejection = Death. It’s Not Being Dramatic. It’s Being Ancient.

Deep inside that beautiful, overthinking cranium of yours is the amygdala—your brain’s own panic button. It doesn’t care about logic. It doesn’t care that you’re a grown adult with bills and responsibilities. It doesn’t care that you’ve read every self-help book on Amazon. Its job is to keep you alive. And to the amygdala, rejection is death.

Why? Because thousands of years ago, getting kicked out of your tribe meant one thing: sabertooth buffet. You were dinner. So your brain learned to do whatever it could to stay liked, approved of, and included.

Fast-forward to 2025. The sabertooths are gone, but your primal wiring hasn’t updated its OS. Now that same life-or-death alarm system fires every time you get left on “read.”

Your logic says “It’s just a no.”

Your amygdala screams “WE’RE GONNA DIE ALONE.”

And so, you freeze. You panic. You go passive. You scroll Instagram, rationalize your inaction, and start “re-strategizing”—translation: hiding.

Creatives, Entrepreneurs, and Misfits: You're Extra Screwed (And Extra Gifted)

You know who feels this most? Creative types. Purpose-driven people. Entrepreneurs. Indie builders. Anyone whose work is personal.

If you’re out here trying to change lives, grow communities, build wealth, or disrupt industries—you’ve probably built your mission with your soul. And when someone says no to your offer, it feels like they’re saying “Your soul sucks.”

Spoiler: they’re not.

But that reaction—that deep ache in your chest, that tunnel-vision, that flood of shame and cortisol—that’s what we’re talking about. That’s your trauma loop lighting up like a Christmas tree.

Even worse? Your imagination joins the party. You start thinking, “Maybe they’re talking crap about me.” Or “Maybe they’re warning others not to work with me.” You craft entire rejection fanfics in your head, and suddenly you’re not just rejected—you’re cancelled.

Reality: they probably didn’t think twice about your message.

Your fantasy: “I’m a fraud and everyone knows it.”

Welcome to the toxic dance of overreaction + imagination. It’s killing your drive, your follow-through, and your bank account.

The Science Is Savage—But It’s Fixable

They’ve studied kids raised in war zones and abusive homes. Their brains literally get rewired for survival. They live in high-alert mode 24/7. They’re not dramatic. They’re adapted. Their nervous systems are conditioned to expect pain, to assume danger, and to preemptively shut down anything that could lead to harm—including new opportunities.

If you grew up in any of the following environments, congrats—you’ve been neurologically trained to fear rejection:

  • Conditional love (You’re only loved when you succeed)
  • Dismissed feelings (“Get over it” was your therapy)
  • High-control parenting or partners (Where you had no agency)
  • Bullying, exclusion, and social rejection (AKA public shaming)
  • Trauma and abandonment (Even subtle, silent forms)
  • Constant criticism, especially in sales, performance, or leadership roles

Now ask yourself—how many of those ring a bell? If you’re in this audience—agency owners, startup founders, side-hustling warriors—I’d bet at least three.

Which means your brain isn't being weak when it fears rejection. It’s being loyal. It’s trying to protect you from perceived danger.

The problem is… the danger isn’t real anymore.

But your brain didn’t get the memo.

“ADD” Isn’t Always a Deficit—It’s an Overload

Ever been so focused on a fear loop you forget what you were doing? That’s not distraction. That’s attention hijacking.

Your brain is being overactive in the wrong places—locked in threat detection. You can’t focus on your follow-up strategy because your mind is running 12 worst-case scenarios about how it might go sideways.

Creatives do this by imagining emotional fallout. Analyticals do this by over-calculating outcomes. Sales pros do this by dissecting every objection like a breakup text.

Either way—it’s hyperactivity, not a lack of capacity.

And if you’ve been told “You’re too sensitive,” what they meant was, “Your nervous system is picking up too many signals at once, and we don’t know how to handle it.”

You are not defective. You are overloaded. And you need to reset.

Exposure Is the Cure—But Not the Old-School Grind Kind

Look—this isn’t about “getting thick skin.” This is about safe exposure to rejection. Controlled, intentional reps. Just like lifting weights—you don’t go bench 300 lbs on day one. You start with 20s and build your reps.

Here’s how:

1. Make low-stakes asks. “Mind if I send you a bit of info?” is a lot easier than “Wanna buy my $25k package today?” Get your reps in with small offers. Desensitize yourself.

2. Practice professional interruption. You’re not being rude by saying “Hey—I just wanted to let you know I help business owners with funding if they ever need it.” That’s not pushy. That’s a service.

3. Normalize no. Collect ten “not interested” responses this week on purpose. Not because you love rejection—but because you need your nervous system to realize nothing bad happens when someone says no.

4. Breathe. (No really.) Try the 4-7-8 breathing method before outreach: Inhale 4, hold 7, exhale 8. Reset your nervous system. Teach your body that outreach doesn’t equal death.

5. Move your body. Walk, stretch, shake it off. Trauma stores in the body. You can’t think your way out of it. Motion beats emotion.

Think This Is “Mindset Woo-Woo”? Think Again.

This isn’t manifesting. This is mechanics. Your rejection sensitivity isn’t about being weak—it’s about being wired. You were taught to fear rejection because rejection used to mean loss, pain, or ridicule.

But now? Rejection means data.

“Not now” is not a life sentence. “No thanks” is not a judgment. And silence isn’t a crime scene.

You’re in a new game. And to win it, you have to update the rules in your head.

The entrepreneurs who win aren’t fearless. They’re perspective-rich. They know how to feel the old fear and do it anyway. Because rejection isn’t a verdict. It’s a variable.

And you? You’re overdue to stop calling your trauma “instinct” and start calling it what it is: false alarms with too much power.
