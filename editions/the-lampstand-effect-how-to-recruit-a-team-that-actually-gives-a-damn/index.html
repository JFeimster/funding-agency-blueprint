The Lampstand Effect: How to Recruit a Team That Actually Gives a Damn

Uses the lampstand metaphor to discuss visible leadership, ethical service, recruitment, and building a team around a shared mission.

Jason Feimster · Moonshine Capital

🔗 Based on DAC’s Agent Training**—'Team Building - Lamstand'**

Let’s face it—your team sucks. Not because they’re bad people. Not because they aren’t talented. But because they’re in it for the wrong damn reason: the money. And here’s the brutal truth—if your business is full of people chasing commissions like junkies after their next hit, it’s only a matter of time before the whole thing burns to the ground.

And you know what? That’s on you.

You recruited them. You led with the sizzle, but forgot the steak. You sold them on “income potential,” “work from home,” and “freedom,” like it was some 2006 MLM fever dream. You whispered sweet nothings about passive income, then handed them a toolkit of high-interest funding options and told them to go fish.

Congratulations. You created an army of debt peddlers instead of solution providers.

But here’s your redemption arc—if you’re brave enough to take it.

It starts with asking a better question:

Who are we really looking for? And why does this business even exist in the first place?

Let’s get into it.

Step One: Burn the Playbook (Then Rewrite It with Actual Integrity)

Every recruitment script you've seen? Trash it.

That “fire your boss” nonsense? It’s bait for desperate people who are about to make your brand a meme on Reddit.

Instead, understand this: you're not looking for salespeople. You’re not building a commission factory. You’re looking for servants—teachers, coaches, real-deal community builders. People who know what it means to go to sleep thinking about how to make someone else’s life easier.

People who lead with mission—not margin.

Because here’s the thing no one tells you at the “opportunity webinar”: if you lead with commission, you’ll always attract mercenaries. And mercenaries don’t build empires—they burn them down when the checks stop clearing.

So let’s talk mission. Let’s talk growth capital. Not “quick fix” loans. Not Band-Aids on bullet wounds. But real financial fuel for businesses that are poised to level up.

Your job isn’t to convince a desperate business owner to take a $200k loan to pay off some late bills.

Your job is to find the owner whose foot is on the gas pedal, who’s trying to scale, who has the vision but not the war chest.

That’s who wins with DAC. That’s who grows. That’s who refers. That’s who remembers your name when they make their first million.

But you’ll never find them if you’re stuck trying to close anyone with a pulse and a pulse rate over 600 on their credit score.

Step Two: Fire the Spotlight, Be the Lampstand

Here’s the cold water to the face: your job isn't to chase people. You’re not the creepy dude at the networking event with 300 business cards and no friends.

You are not a spotlight.

You’re a lampstand.

Your job is to shine.

Jesus said, "You don’t put a lamp under a cloth." So stop hiding your purpose behind clever copy and clickbait. Put it out for the world to see. Show up like a lighthouse for the people who need your help.

When you shine with authenticity, people come to you.

You want to attract the right people? You want a team that doesn’t crumble the second their Facebook ad stops working?

Then be real.

Say this instead: “We help business owners who are ready to grow but can’t get the funding they need from traditional banks. We’re not here to sell debt. We’re here to fuel growth. If that’s something that resonates with you, let’s talk.”

That’s it. That’s the secret sauce.

Step Three: Stop Funding Failure

Let’s take a moment to call out the nonsense.

A client says they’re approved for $200k, and their plan is to use it for “working capital”—a vague euphemism for “we’re drowning and we need a sugar daddy.”

So you hand them $200k. And guess what? They use it to pay off overdue bills and cover payroll for another couple of months. Maybe they even breathe a little.

And then?

Then they’re in the exact same spot, but now they owe $268,000.

You didn’t help them. You handed them a shovel and told them to dig faster.

That’s malpractice, and if you're complicit, you're the villain in this story.

You’re not here to straddle desperate people with debt. You’re here to help business owners create leverage. Fuel the ideas that can generate $200k monthly—not cover up a $200k mistake.

When a client says they’re behind on bills, you say: “What’s your growth plan? What would you do with this money to double your revenue in the next 90 days?”

If they don’t have an answer, you don’t have a deal.

And guess what? They’ll respect you for it. Because nobody else is telling them the truth.

Everyone else is out here with commission breath, foaming at the mouth for a funding bonus.

You? You’re different. You’re building trust. You’re playing the long game. And when they are ready to grow, who do you think they’re calling?

That’s right.

Step Four: Commission is a Byproduct of Mission

Let me repeat that.

Commission is a byproduct of mission.

Get this twisted, and you might make a little money now—but you will never build anything that lasts.

Your brand will be toxic. Your team will rot from the inside. Your clients will regret knowing you.

But if you center your entire operation on serving, then the money follows like a loyal dog.

You make sales. You close deals. But not because you forced it. Because you earned it.

So start recruiting with that in mind. Look for people who are teachers, coaches, mentors, service-minded community builders.

These are the agents who last. These are the ones who light up your Slack channel and don’t ghost after the first 10 rejections.

Find these people. Give them the right playbook. And then watch them shine.

Just don’t forget this—your job isn’t to convince anyone.

Your job is to illuminate.

Illuminate the opportunity. Illuminate the mission. Shine like a damn lampstand.

Recruiting the Right People: If You’re Chasing Warm Bodies, You’re Already Losing

Let’s talk recruiting. Not the corporate HR nonsense. Not the “post and pray” job boards. I’m talking real, grassroots, guerrilla-style recruiting that actually works in the trenches.

Because if you're trying to build a team that’ll help you dominate your city, your region, hell—your niche—then you better stop chasing anyone with a résumé and a pulse. You’re not hiring. You’re building a movement.

And movements don’t happen because you offered a 10% override and a dashboard login.

They happen because you found mission-aligned people. People who give a damn. People who would do it for free (but don’t have to).

Let me be crystal clear: If the words “How much do I make per deal?” are the first thing out of their mouth, you’ve already got a bad apple.

Because the question isn’t what they make.

It’s what they build.

Forget Salespeople. Find Servants.

You’re not building a sales team. You’re building a service squad.

You want to find people who’ve coached Little League for 10 years without ever asking for a dime. People who get out of their cars to help push someone else's through a snowbank. People who sit in the park with a sign that says, “Can we pray for you?” just in case someone’s having a bad day.

You want the teacher who buys supplies out of her own paycheck. You want the high school coach who hasn’t had a raise in 6 years and still shows up early to line the field.

You don’t want the dude who took three affiliate marketing courses and is now “building his brand” in the DMs of 50 people a day.

Find givers. Find lighthouses. And if they also have business sense and B2B experience? Hell yeah. That’s the cherry on top.

Talk to Humans, Not Prospects

When you reach out, don’t sound like a recruitment bot.

This isn’t a funnel. This isn’t a pitch.

It’s a conversation. A real one.

Say something like this:

“Hey, I’m working with a company that helps small business owners get funding when their banks say no. It had me thinking of you—because you’ve always had a heart for helping people in the community.”

“We’re looking to grow our team with people who love serving and mentoring others. Not sales sharks. Teachers. Coaches. Community leaders. That’s who wins here. No cost to join. You just need heart. Want to hear more?”

You’re not begging. You’re not manipulating.

You’re inviting.

And if they don’t vibe with it? Cool. They’re not your people. Move on.

But when you say this to the right person? It hits. It resonates. And now you’ve got a rockstar who’s in it for the right reasons.

The Local Lighthouse Strategy (Why You’re Not Facebook Ads)

Let’s kill a myth right now: You don’t need ads to build this.

You need reach. But more importantly—you need resonance.

Facebook ads might get you 500 leads. But how many of those people give two damns about helping their community?

You know what’s better?

Calling up five teachers in your town and saying:

“You became a teacher to make a difference in kids’ lives. We make a difference in business owners’ lives. Same energy. Different arena. You ever think about a purpose-driven side hustle like that?”

Boom. That’s how you build locally.

Be the lighthouse of your town. Not Instagram’s explore page. Your ZIP code.

Let everyone in your town know: DAC is the spot if you’re a business owner looking for growth capital.

Make it so if someone even mentions the word “funding,” your name comes up in the same breath.

The Math of Impact (Not Income)

Let’s run some numbers.

You build a team of 10 agents in your local area. Each one talks to 5 businesses a week. That’s 50 businesses touched weekly. 200 a month. 2,400 a year.

You think that doesn’t create a ripple?

You think that won’t bring you referrals, credibility, and consistent commissions from actually doing good?

You don’t need 1,000 agents. You need 10 right ones.

Give me 5 mission-minded grinders over 100 half-interested dabblers any day of the week.

Because the math of impact multiplies when the mission is clear. But if the vision is cloudy, all you get is churn.

And churn kills momentum.

Train for Purpose, Not Just Product

So let’s say you’ve got your team. Great. Now what?

Do NOT fall into the trap of just training them on rates, offers, terms, and application processes.

That stuff matters—but only once they understand the why behind the work.

Train them on ethics. On discernment. On when to say no.

Make it clear: we don’t fund bad business models. We don’t put people in worse positions. We are not predatory lenders with a fresh coat of “independent agent” paint.

We fund growth.

We build up.

And if someone’s in a tailspin? We don’t throw them a credit anchor and hope it floats.

The right team gets that. And they thrive because of it.

Let’s Recap the Gospel of Team Building (DAC Style)

  • Don’t lead with commission. Lead with conviction.
  • Don’t recruit sales sharks. Recruit servant-hearted leaders.
  • Don’t push people. Illuminate the opportunity and let them lean in.
  • Don’t rely on ads. Build local authority first.
  • Don’t teach scripts. Teach principles.
  • Don’t tolerate bad fits. Filter ruthlessly for mission alignment.

Because at the end of the day, this isn’t about scaling some faceless sales org. This is about creating a movement of lighthouse leaders who care more about impact than income—and get both because of it.

The Growth Capital Mindset—Why Your Clients Don’t Need a Bailout, They Need a Breakthrough

Let’s take a scalpel to the sacred cow of small business funding: “Working capital.”

It sounds innocent, doesn’t it? Almost responsible. Professional. Like a business owner's way of saying, “We’re doing okay, just need a little float to keep the lights on.”

But let’s get real. When most entrepreneurs say they need “working capital,” what they actually mean is this:

“I’ve made a series of poor financial decisions, and now I’m robbing Peter to pay Paul, and I’m hoping this funding will bail me out long enough to come up with another half-baked plan.”

Sound harsh?

Good. It’s supposed to.

Because your job is not to coddle failure. Your job is to provide leverage to growth-ready businesses—not slap a high-interest bandage on a bullet wound and call it a solution.

Here’s the million-dollar perspective shift: Funding should be a catalyst, not a crutch.

And if that’s not how you or your agents see it, you’re already on the road to resentment—from your clients, your team, and eventually, yourself.

Pain + Debt = More Pain (Stop Being an Enabler)

Picture this.

You’ve got a business owner who’s two months behind on rent. Suppliers are circling like vultures. Payroll’s short, and the panic is setting in.

And then you come along, with your smiling face and your pre-approval letter.

You give them $200K. Sounds heroic, right?

But then the repayment schedule kicks in. They owe $268K.

Now they’re not just behind—they’re bleeding. And you just handed them a credit guillotine.

That’s not growth capital. That’s desperation wrapped in a velvet bow.

And if you’re telling yourself, “Well, they asked for it, I just delivered,” you’re not a consultant—you’re an accomplice.

You need to be the adult in the room. Ask the tough questions.

  • “What’s your plan to grow revenue?”
  • “Will this money actually create ROI, or just buy you time?”
  • “If we don’t fund you today, what’s your next move?”

If their answer is, “I don’t know,” then your answer needs to be, “Then this isn’t the right funding for you.”

Growth Capital is a Business Strategy, Not a Loan

Now let’s flip the script.

You meet a local restaurant owner. One location, always packed. People lining up around the block every Saturday. He’s maxed out seating. Kitchen’s stretched. Service is overwhelmed.

What does he say?

“We’re ready to open a second location, but I don’t want to give up equity. I just need $150K to build it out and launch right.”

Now that’s a funding fit. That’s a growth strategy in action.

Or the retail store owner who’s killing it in sales but knows their Shopify site is garbage. They’ve got the customer base, the demand, the repeat buyers—but their online funnel is leaking like a sieve.

They want $75K to hire a legit digital agency, run paid traffic, and triple their revenue.

Perfect.

That’s what DAC funding was built for—not to save a sinking ship, but to scale one already set to sail.

When you start filtering for these kinds of businesses, your entire mindset shifts. No more commission hunger. No more pressure tactics. Just honest conversations with serious entrepreneurs who know what they’re doing.

You’re no longer “selling money.”

You’re selling acceleration.

Don’t Confuse Movement with Momentum

Here’s a fun fact: 90% of struggling businesses don’t need more money. They need better strategy.

They’re not underfunded. They’re underpriced. Underperforming. Under-market.

You give them $50K, and it’s gone in 30 days—because their funnel is broken. Their offer sucks. Their fulfillment is a nightmare.

So what happens? They default. You lose trust. And DAC gets another black eye in the market.

No thanks.

Which is why it’s your duty to ask:

“Will this funding fix the root problem or just cover the symptoms?”

And if it’s the latter, have the courage to walk away.

Better yet—point them toward actual solutions.

If their marketing sucks, refer them to an agency.

If their pricing model is trash, suggest a consultant.

If their operations are chaos, point them to EOS.

Be a guide, not a goddamn payday lender in a suit.

And when they are ready for capital that multiplies, guess who they’re calling?

When You Say “No,” You Say “I Care”

Saying no to the wrong deal is one of the most powerful trust-building moves you can make.

Because guess what happens next?

That business owner goes to someone else. And that person says yes. Takes the deal. Funds the pain.

And three months later, they’re worse off than before.

Now who looks like the hero?

You.

Because you didn’t push the deal. You didn’t force the pen. You told them the truth—even when it cost you a commission.

That’s what makes you a lampstand. That’s what makes DAC the real deal. That’s what builds a brand that lasts longer than your Facebook group’s engagement spike.

Be the Filter, Not the Funnel

You’re not a machine that cranks out applications. You’re a filter.

You help businesses decide if funding is the right move—not just an available one.

Which means you have to:

  • Say no often.
  • Say yes with clarity.
  • Be okay walking away from “easy money.”
  • And educate your team to do the same.

Because if you build a business based on “yes-men,” you’ll end up surrounded by debt-shovelers, not difference-makers.

And when the market tightens? When clients start ghosting? When the referrals dry up?

You’ll be the only one left holding the bag.

But if you build on truth?

Your reputation becomes recession-proof.

Your client list becomes referral gold.

Your team becomes a movement.

Mission-Driven Agents Don’t Chase, They Shine—How to Stop Convincing and Start Attracting

Let’s put this as bluntly as possible: You are not a spotlight. You’re a damn lighthouse.

Stop chasing. Start shining.

You’ve got too many agents out here running around like confused poodles, barking at every business that even whispers about cash flow problems. It's exhausting. It’s ineffective. And it turns you into a walking, talking “debt pitch.”

No one respects that. Least of all the businesses you’re supposed to be serving.

You’re not a commission-seeking missile. You are the signal in the storm. You’re what business owners see when they’re ready to grow, ready to scale, and sick of hearing “no” from the banks.

So here’s how we fix this broken recruiting machine, one lighthouse agent at a time.

Step 1: Shine So Bright They Can’t Ignore You

There’s this ridiculous idea that if people don’t respond to your pitch, you need to pitch harder.

No. What you need is better energy. Real conviction. The kind that makes someone lean in and go, “Wait, tell me more about what you do.”

That means being visible, yes—but also being credible. Authentic. Purposeful.

When you walk into a room, people should feel what you stand for.

So when someone says, “What do you do?” you don’t answer like a robot: “I help businesses access capital solutions through an independent platform blah blah blah…”

No.

Try this:

“I help growth-minded business owners get funding when the banks tell them no—so they can hire more, scale faster, and stop playing small.”

Boom.

Now they ask the next question. Because you’re not pushing. You’re pulling—with clarity and confidence.

That’s a lampstand.

Step 2: Cut the Desperation Crap

Let’s get this straight: desperation doesn’t sell. It repels.

You walk into a business with sweaty commission breath and the energy of someone who hasn’t closed a deal in three months, they can smell it before you speak.

And they will run.

You want to be respected? Act like you have nothing to prove.

Be the one who walks in, evaluating them as much as they’re evaluating you.

Because you are.

You’re not trying to stuff everyone into a deal. You’re filtering for alignment. For growth-readiness. For sanity.

If someone doesn’t qualify mentally or financially, you don’t cry about it. You smile, shake hands, and move on.

The right clients come back. The wrong ones blame you later.

Step 3: Teach Your Team to Lead Like You

If you’re reading this, you’re probably a founder, a leader, or a manager of some kind. So here’s the truth you don’t want to hear:

Your team reflects your own dysfunctions.

If your people are pitching like sharks, it’s because you trained them—or tolerated them—to do it.

If your people are ghosting leads, burning bridges, or flooding Slack with commission complaints, that’s on you.

Culture isn’t built. It’s modeled.

You want agents who care? Show them what that looks like.

  • Share client wins (that didn’t involve a deal, but built trust).
  • Highlight agents who said no to a bad fit.
  • Celebrate referrals more than revenue.

Teach them: “We’re not here to sell funding. We’re here to illuminate solutions.”

Over time, you’ll start attracting people who mirror that integrity.

And that’s when the magic happens.

Step 4: Be Ruthless About Fit

Not everyone deserves to be on your team.

Read that again.

If someone shows up with that greasy-car-salesman energy, or talks about “closing whales” and “pushing paper,” they are not your people.

Let them go sell solar panels to senior citizens. You’re building something different.

Here’s how you spot the bad apples before they spoil the batch:

  • They ask about pay before purpose.
  • They push hard for deals—even when the math doesn’t add up.
  • They don’t follow up with clients who say no.
  • They only engage when there’s money on the line.

Cut them.

They will create carnage in your brand, and when the fallout hits? You’ll be the one holding the receipts.

Instead, look for agents who:

  • Ask questions about impact, not just income.
  • Follow up with clients even when there’s no deal.
  • Study the DAC platform because they want to serve better.
  • Get more excited about client success stories than their own commissions.

That’s your dream team.

Build that, and you’ll never worry about attrition again.

Step 5: Recruit from Where People Still Have Soul

Let’s get specific.

You want agents with character? Recruit from these pools:

  • Teachers – Underpaid, overworked, servant-hearted.
  • Coaches – Natural leaders. Built to guide. Unafraid of rejection.
  • Ministers/Pastors – Know how to lead with love and truth.
  • Nurses – Empathy + hustle + organizational skill = fire agent.
  • Veterans – Disciplined, loyal, mission-focused.
  • Gig Workers/Creators – They already think like entrepreneurs.

Don’t chase people who want a “side hustle.” Invite those who want a mission.

You’re not asking them to clock in. You’re asking them to join a cause.

And if they get paid doing it? Great. But that’s not why they said yes.

They said yes because they saw a lighthouse—and decided to shine with you.

Becoming a True Market Leader—Why You Must Dominate Locally Before You Scale Globally

Let’s cut the BS: You don’t need to “go viral.”

You don’t need to build a YouTube channel.

You don’t need a slick funnel, 87 email automations, or a podcast featuring CEOs who are “crushing it.”

What you need—what every real leader needs—is local dominance.

The internet is full of ghost gurus flexing six figures from one lucky launch.

But here’s the truth: the strongest businesses in the world were built by people who owned their local block before they ever thought about going national.

So before you recruit agents in five states, or run ads in Seattle from your couch in Tampa, let me ask you one gut-check question:

Does every business within five miles of your house know who you are and what you offer?

If the answer is “no,” put the damn laptop down and start knocking on doors.

You’re not the CEO of a ghost empire. You’re a lighthouse keeper. Your job is to make sure your whole town sees the light before you try to light the whole coast.

How to Build a Local Legacy (Without Being Cringe)

1.) Start with Your Inner Circle

Reach out to every coach, teacher, and business-minded friend you know.

Don’t pitch them. Just share your mission.

“I’m helping local business owners get growth funding when the banks say no. It’s a mission more than a job. You came to mind because you’ve always been a leader in this community. Want to hear how it works?”

Watch what happens. These people respect you. They’ll listen. And some will say yes.

Boom. Now you’re not alone. You’re building your local agent squad.

2.) Map the Businesses in Your Area

Take a Saturday and drive through your neighborhood. Write down every business you pass.

Make a spreadsheet. Include:

  • Business name
  • Industry
  • Owner (if known)
  • Contact method
  • Whether they’ve been contacted

Now you’ve got your personal CRM of potential clients. No guesswork. No excuses.

3.) Be Present—Not Just Online

Buy a branded polo. Print business cards. Get a clean pitch and a clean pair of shoes.

Go out and be seen.

  • Visit local events.
  • Sponsor a little league team.
  • Eat lunch at that taco truck every Tuesday until the owner knows your name.

You want your face burned into the minds of business owners across town.

And when they finally need funding, they won’t Google it—they’ll call you.

4.) Set Up Monthly Meetups

You’re not just an agent—you’re the market leader.

Act like it.

Host a free monthly business meetup at a local coffee shop or library. Nothing fancy. Just business owners, coffee, and conversations.

Topic ideas:

  • “How to Grow Without Giving Up Equity”
  • “Marketing on a Shoestring Budget”
  • “Top 3 Funding Options for Small Businesses”

These don’t have to be pitch fests. In fact, don’t pitch. Just serve.

Eventually, people will ask how you know so much about capital. And that’s your moment.

5.) Recruit from the Trenches

You don’t build a navy from yachts. You build it from dock workers.

Recruit agents who live where you live. People who are already connected.

They’ll bring in clients faster, stay longer, and care more.

Because this isn’t just business to them—it’s personal.

6.) Own the Reputation

Here’s the benchmark:

If someone in your town Googles “business funding,” do you come up?

If not, change that.

  • Get Google reviews from local clients.
  • Set up a Google My Business listing.
  • Ask every client to refer one other business owner.

And if they don’t? Ask why.

Was the experience average? Did the funding not help? Fix it.

Because a true market leader doesn’t just get clients. They get evangelists.

Stop Scaling Crap. Start Scaling Integrity.

Every big empire you admire started with a local legend.

So stop trying to scale a system that’s still wobbly. Nail your town. Dominate your block. Become the go-to growth capital partner for every serious business within 10 miles.

Then scale.

Then run ads.

Then recruit in Seattle.

But until then? Grind where your feet are.

20 FAQs for Entrepreneurs, Builders, and Purpose-Driven Hustlers

  • What exactly is "growth capital" and how is it different from a regular loan? Growth capital is money with a mission. It's not a financial lifeline—it's rocket fuel. It's meant to scale a healthy business, not prop up a sinking one. If you’re borrowing just to stay alive, that’s not growth capital. That’s a delay on your death certificate.
  • Why do so many people misuse business funding? Because no one ever taught them the difference between solving a problem and avoiding pain. They use money like a painkiller, not a strategy. Then they wonder why the headache comes back with interest.
  • What kind of businesses should apply for DAC funding? Businesses with traction, proof of concept, and a clear plan to scale. If you're turning customers away, maxed on capacity, or have a proven offer that needs amplification—you're the ideal candidate. If you're bleeding money and hoping cash fixes your mess? You’re not ready yet.
  • Is it really okay to turn clients away? Hell yes. In fact, it’s your duty. Sending someone into high-interest debt with no ROI plan is not just irresponsible—it’s morally bankrupt. Say no when it’s right, and people will trust you for life.
  • How do I know if an agent is “mission-aligned”? They talk about impact before income. They ask if the funding helps the client before asking what they’ll earn. They’ll follow up even when there’s no commission in sight.
  • Can I recruit people with no finance background? Absolutely. Character over credentials every time. You can teach someone funding. You can’t teach someone how to care. Hire heart, train skill.
  • How much money can I make in this business? That depends on how many people you serve—not how many you sell. Some agents make six figures. Others make six excuses. The difference? Mission over commission.
  • What’s the biggest mistake new agents make? Pitching everyone with a heartbeat. Desperation is not a strategy. Start with filtering, not flooding.
  • How do I become a local market leader? Show up. Serve consistently. Be visible. Be the first name people think of when business funding comes up at the Chamber of Commerce or the local pub.
  • Do I need to run ads? Nope. You need to build presence. Ads can supplement. But if no one in your ZIP code knows your name, you’ve got bigger problems than ad spend.
  • What’s “commission breath” and how do I avoid it? It’s that desperate energy that reeks of “I need this sale to pay rent.” Avoid it by focusing on the client’s win, not your wallet. Ironically, that’s how you make more money anyway.
  • What should I say to someone who’s skeptical? Be honest. Tell them this isn’t for everyone. It’s for people who want to help businesses grow, not just sling capital. That honesty will hit harder than any pitch.
  • What if I live in a small town? Even better. You can own your town. Every business becomes a warm lead, every event becomes a branding opportunity. Small town = high trust = big opportunity.
  • How do I talk to business owners without sounding like a salesman? Lead with curiosity and care. “Hey, just wondering—if you had $100K next week, what’s the first thing you’d do to grow your business?” That opens doors. Not pressure.
  • Can this work as a side hustle? Yes. Just don’t treat it like a hobby. Purpose-driven side hustlers often outperform full-timers who only chase checks. Passion wins.
  • How should I train new agents I recruit? Teach them the why before the how. If they don’t understand the mission, no script will save them. But if they do? They’ll write their own script—and it’ll work better.
  • Should I partner with other local professionals? Definitely. CPAs, real estate agents, insurance brokers—they all know businesses that need funding. Build alliances, not silos.
