The Gospel According to Grind: Why Most Leaders Fail (and How You Won’t)

Discusses leadership standards, protecting time, developing agents, and creating systems and a culture that other team members can repeat.

Jason Feimster · Moonshine Capital

Entrepreneurs, freelancers, side hustlers, agency wolves, and real estate gladiators—lean in. 🔥🔥🔥

You’re not just tired. You’re soul-bruised. Because leadership, despite what Instagram captions and TEDx speakers whisper sweetly in your feed, is not sexy. It’s not coffee shops and laptops with sticker decals. It’s not yachts and “working from Bali.” No, real leadership—the kind that changes lives and legacies—is ugly. It’s frustrating. It's lonely. And it’s time someone finally said it: most people will not follow you where you're going.

But if you’ve got the grit, if you’re sick of wasting time on people who say they’re "in" but ghost you harder than your Tinder matches… read on. Because this isn’t just another motivation fluff piece. This is your leadership gospel. And it’s got blood on it.

The lonely island of leadership isn't just a trope—it’s a damn reality. David Rutz, in the May 19th Leadership Zoom for David Allen Capital (watch it here), channeled his inner apostle Paul, dropped some gospel truth, and laid out exactly why 90% of your “team” will never build a team. Why you’re tired. Why you give time to people who vanish like smoke when it’s time to show up. And why that’s your fault.

Let’s talk about soil. Yeah, Jesus’ kind.

“There are four types of soil,” Rutz says, “and some of your agents are stony ground.”

Translation? You’re planting in trash dirt, expecting it to grow into oak trees.

You bring someone on, pour your heart into them, hand-hold them through onboarding, send them videos, schedule Zooms—and then… radio silence. They didn’t even open the damn welcome email. No LMS, no training. Just “I didn’t know where to click” energy. And you wonder why your blood pressure flirts with the red zone every time they text “so what do I do again?”

David calls them what they are: “engaged leads,” not agents. And here’s the harsh truth—you’ve been calling people your team who haven’t even laced up their boots.

Let’s pause.

Who are you giving your time to? Who gets your 60-minute game plans, your phone calls, your encouragement, your hope—without doing step one?

If you’re being honest, you're probably mentoring dead weight. And no, that’s not noble. That’s just inefficient leadership.

It’s leadership without leverage.

Leadership Stockholm Syndrome

You fall in love with potential. You’re convinced that agent “Jessica” is going to be your team MVP because she said the right things on your first call and sent you three heart emojis in her reply. You decide “Kyle” is going to crush it because he used to sell Cutco and “has that hustle mindset.” And then you spend hours trying to get Jessica to care. You follow up. You resend emails. You send custom videos. You’re basically doing the emotional equivalent of CPR on a corpse.

And the corpse? Still dead.

Now multiply that by five, ten, twenty people. That’s your life now. A one-man mortuary of unrealized dreams, pouring life into people who haven’t earned a single second of it.

David said it plain: “You have to determine who to work with.”

You are not Jesus. And even Jesus didn’t chase the ones who walked away. He moved on. He multiplied with the willing, not the maybe-somedays.

“Addition becomes multiplication,” David reminded us.

You can’t multiply what never even got started.

Here's the test. It’s brutal. It’s simple.

Before you spend more than 10 minutes with any agent on your team, ask yourself:

  • Did they open the welcome email?
  • Did they watch the “Get Started” training?
  • Did they complete the funding training?

If the answer is “no” to any of those, do not do a game plan call. Don’t book the Zoom. Don’t write the custom strategy. Don’t waste another heartbeat.

David didn’t just suggest this; he modeled it after Christ’s strategy. Yeah, we’re going biblical again—because leadership isn’t new. It’s old. And the rules haven’t changed.

You know the parable. Some seed falls on rock. Some seed is eaten by birds. Some gets choked out by weeds. But some—some lands in good soil. You don’t change the seed. You don’t curse the soil. You just plant more. You plant smarter.

Leadership is not about changing people. It’s about identifying soil.

Let’s apply this to business.

Imagine you’ve got 10 potential agents. You spend one hour each on personalized training. That’s 10 hours gone. Eight of them disappear. One becomes a customer service nightmare. One gets a single deal. Cool—you just spent a full workday for one funding. That’s volunteer-level ROI.

Now imagine you filtered. Only gave your time to the two who showed up with open emails and completed trainings. What happens? Exponential. Quality over hope. Certainty over maybe.

Now, here’s where most leaders flinch:

“But what if I miss out on someone who could be amazing if I just kept pouring into them?”

Let me slap that self-sacrificial nonsense out of your brain.

This isn’t about being Mother Teresa. This is about building a legacy business. If someone hasn’t done the bare minimum, they’re not “potential.” They’re pretending. And you owe your real team—the ones who’ve shown up—to not waste yourself on the ones who haven’t.

Rutz put it plainly:

“You’re working from servanthood, and that’s good. But even Jesus didn’t waste time begging people to follow.”

Translation? If they’re not moving, neither should you.

This is the uncomfortable truth you have to swallow if you’re ever going to break past the endless treadmill of recruiting duds, funding one-off clients, and burning out before you ever see real freedom.

Leadership is lonely—but it’s lonelier when you carry people who refuse to walk.

Let go. Reclaim your time. Build a system. Follow the soil.

You’re either fired up or offended. Perfect.

Because real leaders don’t get anywhere being lukewarm. They get there by making decisions that hurt now but pay off forever. They get there by being honest—brutally, consistently, annoyingly honest—with themselves and the people they lead.

And speaking of honesty, let’s talk about something that most people in your shoes ignore until it’s too late:

You’re the Bottleneck—And That’s Why You’re Broke

Yeah, I said it.

If your business is stalling, your commissions are inconsistent, your team is inactive, and your inbox is full of ghosts—you’re the problem. Not your upline. Not the economy. Not your flaky cousin who “totally wants to learn this DAC thing but is really busy right now.”

It’s you.

Because you’ve built your entire leadership model on being needed instead of being duplicated.

Let’s break this down.

The Savior Syndrome

You want to be Superman. You want to feel important. You want people calling you, asking for help, needing your input. It feels good—until you’re buried in low-value conversations with people who don’t execute, can’t follow instructions, and wouldn’t watch a 10-minute training if you paid them.

You’re “leading” people who haven’t even earned a right to your advice.

Let’s call it what it is: an ego trap disguised as leadership.

David hit the nail on the head in the Zoom call:

“There’s a difference between serving people and wasting time on people who aren’t working themselves.”

Boom. There it is.

You think you’re being selfless. You’re actually being selfish. Because helping people who won’t help themselves lets you avoid the harder work of multiplying leaders.

It’s easier to answer 17 DMs about “where do I find my agent link?” than it is to build an actual system where you’re irrelevant to those types of questions.

The Replication Game

Rutz made it clear: You are Paul, and your job is to raise up Timothys.

Not make everyone happy. Not recruit a crowd. Not save every flake who signs up with hope in their eyes and nothing in their follow-through.

Raise. Up. Leaders.

And you can’t do that if you’re the only one who knows how to lead.

If your entire team needs you to function, you don’t have a team—you have hostages.

And if your calendar is full of one-on-one calls with people who haven’t done step one, you’re not building. You’re babysitting.

The real game is this:

How many people on your team can operate without you?

How many people can onboard, fund, recruit, and repeat—without blowing up your phone?

That’s the scoreboard. Everything else is noise.

System > Charisma

Here’s another gut-punch: your energy, charm, charisma, and “I’m so hyped for you” texts are not scalable. Your systems are.

That’s why David kept hammering one thing during the Zoom: the checklist.

You want to build a team that funds clients while you sleep? While you're on a cruise? While you're out hiking and contemplating your entire existence?

You better have a system that:

  • Filters the right people (did they complete the steps?),
  • Hands them a playbook (not “ask me everything”—send them to a video),
  • Equips them to run without you (because you won't always be available).

And this part gets overlooked—badly. Because it’s boring. Systems aren’t sexy. They don’t get likes on Instagram. They’re not dopamine-inducing.

But they build empires.

“We’ve got people getting commission checks with commas in them,” Rutz said.

You think that happened because they were the most talented? The most knowledgeable?

Nope.

It’s because they worked a system that worked when they didn’t.

You don’t need to be perfect. You need to be consistent. You need to be duplicatable. And above all, you need to get out of your own damn way.

Reality Check: Most People Won’t Make It

Now let’s dig into the uncomfortable reality that most recruiters try to sugarcoat:

Most people won’t make it.

Say it out loud. Say it again.

Now tattoo it to your mental forehead.

You will recruit agents who vanish. You will build teams that crumble. You will have people who show early promise and then flake faster than a nervous Tinder date. It’s part of the process.

And guess what? It’s not your job to fix that.

“We’re not for everyone,” David said, and he wasn’t joking.

If Jesus couldn’t recruit his own hometown, what makes you think you’re going to turn every dropout into a superstar?

Stop trying to be a magician. Be a gardener. Plant. Water. Watch.

And when a plant dies, don’t cry over it. Plant more.

The real secret to leadership isn’t endless optimism or unshakable belief in people. It’s the discipline to invest wisely—and cut fast.

Stop Playing the Maybe Game—Play the Money Game

Listen. If your business plan includes the words “maybe,” “hopefully,” or “if they just,” I’ve got news:

You’re building a business on unicorn dust and MLM fairy tales.

And I’m here to slap that sparkle out of your eyes.

Let’s talk about results. Real ones. Measurable. Predictable. Payable. Because entrepreneurship isn’t just a spiritual journey—it’s a revenue sport. And most of you are running onto the field in flip-flops with no game plan, expecting to win championships.

The “Engaged Agent” Myth

Let’s revisit David’s definition of an “engaged agent”:

“They’ve filled out the form. That’s it. Doesn’t mean they’re an agent.”

You hear that? Just because someone signs up doesn’t mean they’re in.

Stop calling every signup a “team member.” That’s like calling someone who googled “how to join the gym” a personal trainer.

Signing up is the easy part. Watching two videos is the first filter. Having the courage to talk to business owners? That’s where the game starts.

Most of your team are spectators wearing team jerseys. They aren’t in the game.

So stop treating them like players. Stop wasting time trying to build playbooks for people who haven’t even put on cleats.

Sorting vs. Saving

If you want real freedom, adopt this mindset immediately:

Leaders don’t save people. They sort them.

Read that again. Tattoo it. Embroider it on your bathroom towels.

You are not a savior. You’re not here to rescue anyone. You’re here to sort people into two categories:

  • Builders – people who take action, get results, and deserve your mentorship.
  • Babysitters – people who need hand-holding, hate responsibility, and treat this like a hobby.

You don’t grow by babysitting. You grow by building with the builders.

Even Jesus didn’t chase the ones who walked away. Why are you?

If someone can’t watch a video and send a message to a business owner, guess what—they’re not “just getting started.” They’re just wasting time.

Recruit Like an NFL Scout

Let’s break this down into business recruiting math. Because this isn’t arts and crafts—it’s scouting.

Every time you ask a team member, “Who do you know that…”

  • “Owns a business?”
  • “Has B2B sales experience?”
  • “Is sick of working 80 hours for peanuts?”
  • “Is posting all the time about being #blessed but is dead broke?”

That’s scouting.

And when your team repeats that process, that’s when addition becomes multiplication.

You’re not supposed to recruit everyone. You’re supposed to recruit through everyone.

Stop looking for unicorns. Look for workers.

“Even someone who’s been in the business for less than a week can land a deal,” David said.

Boom. Proof.

Experience is a crutch. It’s behavior that matters.

The Hustle Is in the Filter

This is where most side-hustlers go wrong. They confuse hustle with help. They think that grinding 12 hours with the wrong people is “hard work.”

That’s not hustle. That’s desperation.

The real hustle? Filtering fast. Talking to more people. Getting more no’s.

Let me say it for the people still whispering affirmations into their vision boards: You do not need your team to like you. You need them to execute.

That means:

  • Filtering the dead weight,
  • Duplicating what works,
  • Scaling through systems.

You think you're stuck because you're not good enough. No. You’re stuck because you’re babysitting dreamers instead of building with doers.

The “I’ll Just Get My First Client First” Lie

This one’s deadly.

You’re telling yourself you’ll “start recruiting after you close your first deal.”

Really? You want to prove yourself to yourself before you help someone else win?

Newsflash: that’s not leadership. That’s insecurity wrapped in productivity cosplay.

David said it best:

“You don’t have to be the expert. You’re working with experts.”

You can leverage your upline. Use their results. Use their energy. Use their Zooms. Use the tools. You don’t need to become the oracle—you just need to become the connector.

Let your enroller do the heavy lifting while you focus on introductions.

Success loves speed. Delay is death.

Want to Earn Real Freedom? Get Ruthless.

This business isn’t about feelings. It’s about fruit.

You want a passive income stream that spits checks with commas? You better start acting like a damn investor. Investors don’t keep pouring money into bad stock. They cut losses and double down on ROI.

Same rules apply.

  • Have a filter.
  • Stick to it.
  • Stop justifying laziness from people with nice personalities.

You don’t build financial independence by being polite. You build it by being precise.

This is your permission slip to say “no” to underperformers. To stop apologizing for protecting your time. To stop explaining the basics 14 times.

You’re not a guru. You’re not a hotline. You’re not a one-person motivational poster.

You’re a damn leader.

Act like it.

You Are the Culture—So Quit Building a Circus

You ever walk into a business and feel the mediocrity in the air? That weird funk of “everyone here’s just doing enough not to get fired”?

Yeah. That starts with leadership.

Your culture isn’t defined by your mission statement. It’s defined by what you tolerate.

And if your team’s culture is:

  • Excuses over execution,
  • Training without traction,
  • “Community” without commission…

…that’s on you, boss.

So, let's build a culture that slaps.

Rule #1: Culture is Not Cuddly

If your Zooms feel like a therapy session for lost dreamers instead of a war room for killers, you’ve lost the plot.

This isn’t a daycare. This is a commission-only Navy SEAL team.

David said it like this:

“If something happened to me tomorrow, would you have the boldness to carry on the teachings?”

That’s the culture test. If your team couldn’t operate without your weekly pep talks, you don’t have a culture. You have a cult of personality.

And when you burn out (you will), they’ll vanish (they will).

You want a business that lives without you? Build a culture of execution.

Here’s what that looks like:

  • First team meeting: “Welcome. If you’re not willing to watch training, you don’t get access to my time.”
  • Second meeting: “Did you get your first funding yet? No? Great. Here’s the plan. You’ve got 7 days.”
  • Third meeting: “We don’t do hand-holding here. We do breakthroughs. Let’s go.”

That’s leadership. That’s culture. That’s why teams scale.

Not because you created a Slack channel with cute emojis.

Rule #2: The Standard is the Standard

You can’t build a championship team if your expectations change based on who’s in the room.

David was clear: same hour, different outcomes.

You give two agents the same training, same love, same support. One produces. The other vanishes.

That’s not your failure. That’s the filter doing its job.

What is your failure? Letting the non-producer slide because they’re “trying.”

Let me be blunt: “trying” is code for “stalling.”

In the real world, trying doesn’t pay bills. Doing does.

If your culture rewards effort over output, you’ll get neither.

The standard must be this:

  • Complete the basics, or you don’t get mentored.
  • Show up, or you don’t get noticed.
  • Deliver results, or stop asking for time.

High standards don’t push people away—they pull the right ones in.

Rule #3: Build for Depth, Not Hype

Let’s say you get 20 new agents this month. Great. Everyone’s excited. Chat’s blowing up. Fire emojis everywhere.

But now it’s week three. Only three of them did anything. One closed a deal. Two are ghosting. The rest are reposting memes about “manifesting millions” while binge-watching TikToks.

You start to panic.

You launch another motivation call. You send longer texts. You write personal pep talks. You even start lowering the bar—just to keep people “engaged.”

Congratulations. You just destroyed your culture.

David broke it down simply: “Only work with those who are working themselves.”

So here’s how you fix it:

  • Stop counting sign-ups. Start counting steps completed.
  • Stop tracking conversations. Track contracts funded.
  • Stop being impressed by energy. Get obsessed with execution.

You’re not here to build a crowd. You’re here to build a core.

That starts with depth. Not hype.

Rule #4: Your Culture is Only as Strong as Your Worst Agent

Let that slap land.

Your team’s culture is not set by your best. It’s set by your worst—the one you still tolerate.

You know the one.

  • Always late to Zooms.
  • Asks questions that were answered in the first video.
  • Talks big, does nothing.
  • Whines in group chats. Creates confusion. Pulls attention like an emotional black hole.

And you let them stay. Because you’re scared to lose “momentum.” You don’t want to seem “mean.”

You just nuked your own credibility.

Real leaders cut cancer fast.

If someone’s not working, not growing, not contributing—they’re subtracting. And your silence signals approval.

You think you’re being kind. You’re just sabotaging the builders.

So clean house. Set standards. Make quitting easier than dragging.

Your real team—the ones you need—will thank you for it.

Rule #5: Multiply Values, Not Just Actions

Let’s bring this home with some legacy talk.

If all your team does is duplicate what you do, you’ll build a machine.

But if they duplicate what you believe? You’ll build a movement.

David talked about it through the lens of scripture—Paul passing the torch to Timothy. Not just the actions. The boldness, the conviction, the mission.

That’s the final unlock.

What you value is what your team becomes.

So what do you really value?

  • Consistency or charisma?
  • Training or talent?
  • Numbers or names?

Build that into everything: your trainings, your conversations, your Zooms, your onboarding.

When your values bleed through your system, your system gets teeth.

And your culture gets real.

Stop Thinking in “Ranks”—Start Thinking in “Roots”

You’re obsessed with rank. And why wouldn’t you be? It’s how this industry grooms you: get to Senior Field Blah Blah, chase the next bonus, qualify for the trip, scream from the stage.

Cool.

But none of that matters if your team falls apart when you're not watching.

You need roots. Roots grow in the dark. Roots hold things down. Roots outlive storms and outproduce hype.

Because you planted well.

Teach like Paul. Raise Timothys.

That means:

  • Systems over speeches.
  • Documentation over delegation.
  • Structure over sentiment.

You’re not here to be impressive. You’re here to build the kind of tree people can sit under.

Focus on the One—Not the Many

Let’s shatter another myth:

You don’t need hundreds of people to be rich. You need one builder who builds five more.

Addition is sexy. Multiplication is wealth.

Rutz dropped a number: if every agent you bring in nets you an average $100 override per funded deal, and your team does 10 deals a week? That’s $1,000/week just in override. No personal clients. No side hustles. Just multiplication.

But here’s the secret:

You don’t get 10 deals a week by managing 100 people.

You get it by raising five who become you.

That’s the filter now. When you talk to a new agent, ask yourself:

Can this person replace me?

If yes: pour in. If no: plug them into the system and step back.

Legacy doesn’t come from doing more. It comes from building others who can do without you.

Show Up with Scar Tissue, Not Just Scripts

Real leaders bleed.

They don’t just preach process. They preach pain. Because that’s what people remember. That’s what duplicates.

David didn’t just teach on Zoom. He dropped stories about Paul, about betrayal, about loneliness. About planting in the wrong soil. About seeing people vanish right when you needed them most.

That’s not weakness. That’s power.

Your team doesn’t need a perfect leader. They need a real one.

So if you want to build loyalty, build transparency.

  • Own your ghosted weeks.

This makes you human. Humans are followable. Gods aren’t.

Build with blood. Build with scars. Build with story.

⚔️ 20 No-BS FAQs for Leaders Who Actually Want to Win

  • What’s the biggest mistake new leaders make when building a team? They confuse signups with starters. Just because someone joins doesn’t mean they’re committed. Stop assigning potential to people who haven’t earned it.
  • How soon should I start recruiting if I’m brand new myself? Immediately. You don’t need to be the expert—just the inviter. Leverage your upline and tools while you learn. Delay is just fear in disguise.
  • What’s the bare minimum an agent must do before I invest my time? Open the welcome email. Watch the Get Started and Funding training. If they haven’t done those, they’re not ready. Period.
  • What do I say to someone who keeps “thinking about it”? You say: “Totally cool. But I only have time to mentor people who are moving. When you're ready to take action, hit me up.”
  • Should I motivate my team with prizes and contests? You can, but they’re sprinkles on the cake. The cake is systems, standards, and personal accountability. Don’t bribe adults to work.
  • What if someone starts strong and then disappears? Let them. People vanish all the time. Bless and release. You’re not here to chase ghosts—you’re here to plant seeds in fertile soil.
  • Should I do one-on-one calls with every agent? Only with the ones who’ve earned it. That’s not elitist—it’s strategic. Protect your energy for the doers.
  • How do I keep myself from burning out? Systems. Boundaries. Filters. You’re not a machine. You’re a multiplier. Treat your time like it's money—because it is.
  • How do I scale without losing the personal touch? Use tech and team. Record high-quality trainings. Empower leaders. Use group chats, calls, and duplicatable playbooks.
  • What if my upline isn’t helping me? Skip them. Go upline until you find someone who’s building. Or build your own system and become that person for others.
  • How do I know someone is serious? They show up. They do the steps. They ask high-level questions. They make decisions fast and don’t need babysitting.
  • Should I give second chances to non-performers? Sure, once. Maybe twice. But if someone keeps choosing excuses over execution? Time to cut.
  • What does a high-performing agent actually do daily? Contacts business owners. Follows up on leads. Recruits others. Trains new people. Tracks their own performance. Period.
  • Can I lead without being loud or extroverted? Hell yes. Leadership is not about volume. It’s about clarity, consistency, and conviction. Quiet power builds empires.
  • What should I do when people ask for help but never implement? Stop helping. Direct them to the system. If they won’t use the system, they won’t use your advice either.
  • How much should I rely on company Zooms to train my team? Use them—but don’t outsource leadership. Eventually, you should host your own. That’s how you scale speed and culture.
  • What’s the difference between a team and a movement? A team follows instructions. A movement owns the mission. You create movements by duplicating values, not just tasks.
  • How do I stay motivated when my team isn’t producing? Zoom out. Focus on what you control: your activity, your consistency, your growth. Momentum is an inside job.
  • When should I start doing my own presentations? When you can say “I’ve watched this three times, I know the structure, and I’m ready to lead.” Don’t wait for permission.
  • What does successreallylook like in this game? It’s waking up to commissions you didn’t chase. It’s leading people who lead people. It’s disappearing from the spotlight and watching your team still shine.
